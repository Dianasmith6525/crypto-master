Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Policy

Effective Date: January 1, 2025

1. Introduction and Purpose

Cryptocurrency Trading Platform ("Company") is committed to maintaining the highest standards of regulatory compliance. This Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Policy establishes procedures to prevent, detect, and report money laundering and terrorist financing activities. We comply with regulations established by FinCEN, FATF, and other relevant authorities.

2. Regulatory Framework

Our AML program is based on compliance with:

  • Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and implementing regulations
  • Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020
  • FATF Recommendations for combating money laundering
  • Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) regulations
  • Customer Due Diligence (CDD) requirements
  • Know Your Customer (KYC) regulations
  • Beneficial Ownership regulations

3. Know Your Customer (KYC) Program

3.1 Customer Identification Program (CIP)

We collect and verify the following information from all customers:

  • Full legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Social Security Number or Tax ID
  • Current residential address
  • Government-issued photo ID verification
  • Facial recognition verification

3.2 Beneficial Ownership Verification

For business accounts, we verify:

  • Company registration documents
  • Articles of incorporation or formation
  • Identification of all beneficial owners (25%+ ownership)
  • Business purpose and source of funds
  • Authorized signatories

3.3 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

For high-risk customers, we perform enhanced due diligence including:

  • Public figure/PEP screening
  • Enhanced source of wealth verification
  • Additional documentation requirements
  • Ongoing monitoring
  • Third-party verification services

4. Customer Risk Classification

All customers are classified into risk categories:

Low Risk

  • Individual retail customers with normal transaction patterns
  • Domestic financial institutions
  • Established legitimate businesses

Medium Risk

  • International customers
  • Businesses in high-risk industries
  • Customers with unusual transaction patterns
  • High-volume traders

High Risk

  • PEPs (Politically Exposed Persons)
  • Customers from high-risk jurisdictions
  • Sanctioned individuals or entities
  • Entities in high-risk industries (casinos, money services)
  • Customers with suspicious transaction patterns

5. Sanctions Compliance (OFAC)

We maintain strict compliance with OFAC sanctions lists:

  • Screen all new customers against SDN (Specially Designated Nationals) list
  • Ongoing screening of existing customers
  • Automatic blocks on transactions involving sanctioned individuals/entities
  • Immediate reporting of violations to FinCEN
  • Maintenance of blocked funds records

6. Transaction Monitoring

6.1 Suspicious Activity Detection

We monitor transactions for suspicious patterns:

  • Structuring (layering transactions to avoid thresholds)
  • Rapid deposits and withdrawals
  • Unusual transaction sizes or frequency
  • Transactions inconsistent with customer profile
  • Transactions to/from high-risk jurisdictions
  • Round-dollar transactions
  • Cash-intensive business patterns

6.2 Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR)

Suspicious transactions are reported to FinCEN within 30 days via Suspicious Activity Reports (SAR). We maintain records of all SARs filed.

7. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

7.1 Ongoing CDD

We conduct ongoing CDD to:

  • Understand normal customer transaction patterns
  • Identify changes in account activity
  • Verify information remains current
  • Detect emerging risk factors
  • Update customer profile data

7.2 Customer Information Updates

We periodically request customers update their information. Failure to comply may result in account restrictions or closure.

8. Restricted Jurisdictions and Entities

We do not provide services to customers from or conducting business with:

  • OFAC-sanctioned countries
  • Crimea region
  • Designated terrorist organizations
  • Listed individuals and entities
  • High-risk jurisdictions with insufficient AML controls

9. Reporting and Record Keeping

9.1 Currency Transaction Reports (CTR)

We file CTRs for transactions exceeding $10,000 as required by federal law.

9.2 Record Retention

We maintain records for minimum 5 years:

  • Customer identification documents
  • Transaction records
  • SARs and CTRs filed
  • Audit trails
  • Compliance documentation

10. Staff Training and Compliance

All employees receive:

  • Initial AML training upon hire
  • Annual refresher training
  • Role-specific compliance training
  • Updates on regulatory changes
  • Red flag and reporting procedures

11. Third-Party Risk Management

We assess AML compliance of service providers including:

  • Payment processors
  • Identity verification services
  • Banking partners
  • API providers

12. Compliance Officer Responsibility

Our Compliance Officer oversees:

  • AML policy implementation
  • Regulatory compliance
  • SAR/CTR filing
  • Staff training
  • Third-party oversight
  • Annual compliance testing

13. Enforcement Actions

Violation of this AML Policy may result in:

  • Transaction restrictions
  • Account suspension
  • Account closure and fund forfeiture
  • Reporting to authorities
  • Legal action

14. Changes and Updates

This policy is reviewed and updated annually or as regulatory requirements change. Updates will be effective upon posting.

15. Contact and Questions
